Product scope

OpenPOS is a local Windows order-entry and sales-record application for a small shop, with a configurable catalog and optional kitchen clients. The first release supports a pizza shop and ordinary retail items in the same menu. The requested stack is preserved: TypeScript, React, Electron, Webpack, Yarn, JSON business files, NDJSON logs and an NSIS Windows installer.

The word “POS” does not imply payment processing. There is no integrated tender capture, card terminal, processor settlement, cash drawer protocol, accounting certification or proof-of-payment workflow. Receipts describe orders and totals. These exclusions are visible in the business handbook and product overview.

Windows 10 x64 and newer are the target platform. Node is a development dependency, not something end users need to install for a packaged Electron application. A working product should be verified on the intended Windows devices, printers and private network before live use.

Money and tax

Decision Behavior and reason
Integer minor units Persist prices and totals as integer cents. The UI accepts normal decimal prices and converts at the boundary. This avoids accumulated binary floating-point errors for ordinary two-decimal currencies.
One configured tax percentage 13 means 13%, not 0.13. Compute tax on item subtotal plus taxable selected fees and round once to the nearest cent.
Explicit fee selection Fees are reusable named configuration entries with an amount and taxable flag. Staff select applicable fees per order; fulfillment does not silently add a surcharge.
Full variant prices A variant’s price is its full selling price. It does not add to the base price.
Historical snapshots Order lines copy the selected name, unit price and recipe. Orders also store their selected fees and tax context. A later menu edit does not alter already saved order lines.
Gross has a precise meaning Audit gross is the total of completed orders, including fees and tax. Item revenue excludes fees and tax. Cancelled and unfinished orders are excluded from completed sales. Sales use completion dates; stock uses movement dates. Date boundaries use the authoritative standalone/master computer’s local timezone.

Supported currencies are CAD, USD, EUR, GBP, AUD and NZD. Changing currency is blocked while any order history exists, because relabeling saved nominal amounts would misrepresent the records. Choose currency after Start fresh clears demo orders and before recording real sales. No currency conversion is performed. Zero-decimal and three-decimal currency rules, multiple tax rates, per-product tax exemptions, inclusive tax, discounts, tipping and refunds require explicit extensions rather than being inferred from the first release.

Orders and inventory

Completion is the stock boundary

The inventory requirement ended mid-sentence in section A; the surrounding acceptance criteria make completion the consistent deduction point. Saving an in-progress order does not consume stock. Completing an order consumes its snapshotted recipe. Auto-complete executes the same transition immediately.

Completed-order editing would otherwise double-deduct stock or lose the old consumption. The implementation compares previous and new consumption and records the delta. Cancellation is an added correction workflow: it preserves the order, excludes it from current sales totals and reverses recorded consumption.

Variant recipe overrides and inheritance

A nonempty variant recipe is its complete recipe, not an addition to the base recipe. An empty variant recipe inherits the base recipe. This lets common ingredient consumption be defined once, with explicit overrides for different sizes. For an untracked variant alongside tracked variants, leave the base recipe empty and define each tracked option explicitly. Basic retail items use one recipe component with quantity 1.

Snapshot before catalog changes

The sale captures its selected product information. Otherwise changing a pizza recipe tomorrow could alter the stock calculation for an order entered today. Existing order lines keep their snapshots during edits; newly selected items use the current catalog.

Negative inventory is allowed

Stock can go below zero and low-stock thresholds provide visibility. Blocking a customer order because of an unrecorded stock delivery would be disruptive. The tradeoff is that stock is an operational estimate requiring reconciliation, not a guaranteed availability reservation.

No stock reservation

In-progress orders do not reserve ingredients. Cancellation assumes returned inventory unless the operator records a separate stock adjustment; waste and preparation yield are not inferred. Each completed order records whether inventory was enabled at its completion, so toggling the setting later does not retroactively invent or discard historical consumption.

Files and durability

Shared files are menu.json, inventory.json, orders.json, config.json and movements.json. Local device and password data are kept separately. metadata.json tracks schema/revision; transaction.json carries pending recovery. client-cache.json stores a client’s last accepted master snapshot, separate from its standalone data. instance.lock prevents two processes opening the same folder. Business events use events.ndjson; desktop diagnostics use logs/application.ndjson.

Serialized, recoverable writes

A business mutation can change an order, inventory quantities and movement history together. The store serializes updates and uses an application-managed recovery transaction around its multi-file writes. Recovery is completed before normal operation resumes. Individual files remain readable JSON. A failed write disables further mutations until restart recovery. A repeated order-creation request carries a stable ID to avoid creating a duplicate after a retry.

Manual changes are untrusted input

The menu file is watched, parsed and validated before adoption. Malformed or conflicting edits are preserved under backups, rejected with a warning, and leave the last accepted state in memory. Menu editing is the supported hot-reload use case; other JSON files are not live-edit interfaces. Routine order and stock changes should use application commands so revisions, totals and movement history stay coherent.

Per-machine install, per-user data

The installer is per machine, but the explicitly requested .openpos directory is under the current user’s profile. Creating it on first application launch avoids placing the data in an administrator account used for installation. Two Windows users on one physical computer therefore have separate data, passwords and device settings.

Demo and production separation

A new store is seeded with mixed pizza and retail data, inventory and completed/in-progress orders. Development runs use a repository-local .dev-data directory so development does not modify the ordinary shop folder. The administrative Start fresh workflow creates a backup, removes sample orders and movement history, and sets inventory balances to zero while preserving the catalog and business configuration. It requires the explicit phrase START FRESH.

This is also a destructive live-shop action if used after real trading. The explicit confirmation and automatic backup make its consequences reviewable; it is not performed silently on installation or update. Business backup exports omit local device/auth files and have no one-click restore UI; a complete closed-app folder backup is the documented full-device recovery method.

One authoritative master

The requested ability for every device to edit an order is compatible with a single authority. Each client submits commands to the master; the master validates, saves and distributes authoritative state. Clients do not independently merge file copies. This gives the kitchen editing access without a multi-master conflict algorithm.

  • Three device roles: standalone, master and client. The same binary serves all roles.
  • Explicit client registration: the master stores permitted client identifiers. A shared sync secret authenticates requests.
  • Signed communication: HMAC signatures authenticate requests and responses. Timestamps within 60 seconds and unique nonces guard against straightforward request replay.
  • Conflict detection: expected revisions reject edits made against stale state. Staff reopen the updated record and retry deliberately.
  • Refresh protocol: clients poll each second with their known revision. Unchanged state returns a small acknowledgement; changed state returns an authoritative snapshot. Request bodies are limited to 2 MB and responses to 64 MB.
  • No offline writes: when the master is unavailable, a client cannot commit changes. Offline queues and automatic conflict resolution are deliberately excluded.
  • Device-local preferences: the default tab, role, connection details and local administrator session are not business data replicated from the master.

The master must have a configured administrator password before hosting sync. LAN requests use HTTP, not TLS. Message authentication does not hide menu, order or customer information from someone observing the network. Use a trusted private network and no direct internet port forwarding.

A shared key identifies a trusted installed client, not an individual employee. Anyone with access to a device’s files or sync key sits outside the application’s UI-only access boundary. Individual staff identities, central permissions and cryptographic audit trails are future architecture work.

Desktop and administrator boundaries

The renderer runs with context isolation and without Node integration. The preload bridge exposes a small typed API instead of arbitrary IPC or filesystem access. Business input is validated again where trusted operations occur; a form’s HTML validation alone is not a sufficient boundary.

The local administrator password is stored as a salted scrypt hash. A fresh standalone instance has setup access until its first password is set. The unlocked session is held in memory; locking or restarting revokes it once a password exists. Audit and administrative mutations are protected beyond simply hiding their buttons. Staff may still create, edit, complete and cancel orders.

A connected client verifies the master’s administrator password and receives a client-bound token with an eight-hour expiry. Locking, a password change, or a master restart invalidates it. The client’s retained local installation password can unlock connection repair while disconnected, but cannot authorize offline business mutations or bypass the master. Because HTTP is unencrypted, the unlock password must be used only on a trusted private network.

This interpretation resolves the installation criterion about hiding Audit in the kitchen: administrator access is enabled by unlocking that device. It is not a persistent global setting that unlocks all clients at once. There is no permanent employee account database or per-user permission matrix.

Local JSON files and exports are not encrypted. Protect access with Windows accounts and disk encryption where appropriate. Backups contain business and possibly customer data. Diagnostic logging should avoid passwords and sync secrets.

Interface and distribution

The visual design prioritizes a calm, high-contrast work surface, large touch targets, readable prices, clear order status and fast category selection. Material Design Icons cover standard actions. A small repository-native vector mark provides app identity without adding a remote-image dependency.

The primary workspace has a wide menu pane and a narrower current-order pane. Menu Items, Orders and Audit are top-level navigation destinations. Administrative dialogs share create/edit fields so catalog changes appear immediately and users do not need to learn separate maintenance paths.

Help is a self-contained HTML handbook with local styling, opened in the user’s default browser. Architecture, decisions, acceptance mapping, sales collateral and the preserved request remain source-only. No network font or CDN stylesheet is required.

NSIS packaging creates a Windows x64 installer with desktop and Start menu shortcuts. The application creates its data directory at runtime. Code signing is not configured by default; distribution owners must supply their own signing credentials and perform platform release checks.

Validation strategy

Verification is layered around behavior that matters: money rounding, fees and taxes; required field validation; variant pricing; order lifecycle; edit/cancel stock adjustments; report aggregation; JSON reload and recovery; admin protection; and master/client propagation and conflict handling. Renderer checks exercise representative user workflows in the actual Electron app.

yarn typecheck checks the shared contracts and process boundaries. yarn test runs unit and integration checks. yarn test:e2e checks the desktop workflow. yarn build verifies compilation and resource copying, while yarn dist exercises installer generation. See the repository test output for executed results; this document describes the required strategy rather than pretending every future machine is validated.

Release verification still belongs on the target hardware.

Exercise installation and upgrade with a standard Windows account, touch input on the target tablet, the actual receipt printer, two physical devices and the intended private firewall rules. Keep a recoverable backup before testing upgrades against live data.

Known boundaries and useful next steps

  • Operational capacity: JSON snapshots and one master suit small shops. The Orders screen renders the first 50 matching cards per status, with controls to load another 50. Counts and filters still cover the full in-memory order collection; this bounds initial card rendering rather than changing the persistence architecture. No large-business concurrency or dataset-size benchmark is claimed. Local JSON files are limited to 64 MB each; finite row limits also apply. Add database-backed persistence and measured performance targets before promising high-volume multi-location operation.
  • Resilience: the master is a dependency for clients. There is no automatic failover or offline order reconciliation.
  • Financial scope: no payment processing, cash tender/change workflow, processor refunds, fiscal-device integration, tax certification or accounting export contract.
  • Retail scope: no barcode scanner-specific integration, weighted scale integration, loyalty program or customer account balances.
  • Restaurant scope: no table map, split bill, tipping, course timing, delivery dispatch or third-party delivery integration.
  • Inventory scope: no reservation, recipe cost accounting, supplier purchasing, expiry management or formal waste ledger.
  • Security scope: no TLS configuration, user-by-user roles, encrypted local data or tamper-proof audit trail.
  • Distribution scope: no invented public download, automatic updater or preconfigured signing certificate. Build the installer locally and publish through the project’s chosen release process.

These boundaries do not alter the implemented acceptance criteria. They make the first version’s operational model explicit and keep the sales material honest.
